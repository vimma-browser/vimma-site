Tabs, panes and workspaces are driven by ctrl+b, tmux's prefix: press ctrl+b, let go, then press one more key. Firefox's tab strip is hidden; the status bar shows where you are (tab 2/5), and ctrl+b g searches every tab when you need to find one.

How the prefix works

  1. Press ctrl+b and let go. The status bar shows prefix.
  2. Press the command key, such as c for a new tab.

There is no time limit between the two, and Esc cancels. The prefix works in every mode, in the URL bar and while you type in a field, and the page never sees it. Prefer ctrl+a? Set prefix in config.toml.

Tab keys

ToPress
Open a new tabctrl+b c or t

Go to tab 1 to 9ctrl+b 1 … ctrl+b 9, or 3gt for tab 3

t, gt and x are the normal-mode shortcuts; the ctrl+b forms work everywhere. Tab numbers and next / previous stay within the current workspace.

Find a tab

ctrl+b g (or ctrl+shift+a, b, or :ls) opens tab search, ready to type. It finds any tab: every workspace of this window, your other windows, and the tabs and windows you closed recently. Type a few letters of the title, address or tab name; every letter goes into the search, including j, k and q. ctrl+n and ctrl+p (or Down and Up) move, Enter goes to the tab, its window and its workspace, or reopens a closed one, and Esc closes the search. :tabsearch text opens it with the text already typed.

Rename a tab

ctrl+b shift+t asks for a name at the bottom of the window. The name replaces the page title in the status bar and tab search, and survives a restart. An empty name brings the title back.

The side panel

The side panel lists the current workspace's tabs down the left of the window, so you can see what is open and tidy it up with a few keys. It is closed until you ask for it, and closes again when you pick a tab.

Close the highlighted tab (the panel stays open: x x x closes three)x

While the panel is open, plain keys are the panel's; ctrl+b keys and : commands still work. A tab playing sound shows ♪. The rest of its keys are in the Side panel table below.

Show tabs together

  1. Open the panel with ctrl+b b.
  2. Press space on each tab you want to see side by side, for example your editor, the docs and a preview. Marked rows show ●.
  3. Press Enter. The marked tabs become the workspace's panes, in its layout, and no page reloads.

With a split already open, the panel opens with its tabs marked: unmark one and press Enter to take it out of the split, or mark another to add it.

The status bar

The window's last line is always there, except while a page such as a video is in fullscreen. From the left it shows the mode badge, any keys you have started (prefix after ctrl+b), the workspace (● 2:work), the tab (tab 2/5) and in a split the pane (pane 2/3), then the page title or your name for the tab. On the right: a search in the page (/word [2/5]), a connected agent (◇ agent), a blocked pop-up, a running download (↓ 42%), and the page's address. A narrow window drops the least important parts first; the mode badge always stays. Every part is listed under Details.

More in the user guide

These parts of the window work from the keyboard too, and are described in Vimma's user guide on GitHub:

  • Downloads: ctrl+b d (or :downloads) opens the downloads picker, newest first.
  • Site permissions: :site lists what the site in this tab may do (camera, microphone, location, notifications and the rest) and changes it.
  • Blocked pop-ups: answer Firefox's pop-up bar with y to allow the site's pop-ups or n to block them.
  • Hiding the toolbar: ctrl+b u (or :toolbar) hides Firefox's toolbar, leaving the status bar as the address line. The same section covers answering permission prompts and dialogs with y and n.

See every key

ctrl+b ? lists every binding over the page. Press / and type to filter it; Esc or q closes it. Keys changed in your config.toml are marked *.

Firefox's own keys

Most of them still work: ctrl+t, ctrl+shift+t, ctrl+tab, alt+1…alt+9 (cmd+1…cmd+9 on macOS), ctrl+l, ctrl+f and the rest. They are Vimma bindings with Firefox's meaning, so ctrl+b ? lists them and config.toml can rebind them. The few that sit on keys Vimma uses are switched off, so no key means two things: close a tab with x rather than ctrl+w, and open Vimma's bookmarks with ctrl+b shift+b. Firefox's shortcuts has the list, and how to change it.
