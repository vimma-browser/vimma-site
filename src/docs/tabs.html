Tabs, panes and workspaces are driven by ctrl+b, tmux's prefix: press ctrl+b, let go, then press one more key. Firefox's tab strip is hidden; the status bar shows where you are (tab 2/5), and ctrl+b g searches every tab when you need to find one.

How the prefix works

  1. Press ctrl+b and let go. The status bar shows prefix.
  2. Press the command key, such as c for a new tab.

There is no time limit between the two, and Esc cancels. The prefix works in every mode, in the URL bar and while you type in a field, and the page never sees it. Prefer ctrl+a? Set prefix in config.toml.

Tab keys

ToPress
Open a new tabctrl+b c or t
Next / previous tabctrl+b n / ctrl+b p, or gt / gT
Go to tab 1 to 9ctrl+b 1 … ctrl+b 9, or 3gt for tab 3
Close the tabctrl+b shift+x or x
Rename the tabctrl+b shift+t
Search every tabctrl+b g, ctrl+shift+a or b

t, gt, x and b are the normal-mode shortcuts; the ctrl+b forms work everywhere. Tab numbers and next / previous stay within the current workspace.

Find a tab

ctrl+b g (or b, or :ls) opens tab search, ready to type. It finds any tab: every workspace of this window, your other windows, and the tabs and windows you closed recently. Type a few letters of the title, address or tab name; every letter goes into the search, including j, k and q. ctrl+n and ctrl+p (or Down and Up) move, Enter goes to the tab, its window and its workspace, or reopens a closed one, and Esc closes the search. :tabsearch text opens it with the text already typed.

Rename a tab

ctrl+b shift+t asks for a name at the bottom of the window. The name replaces the page title in the status bar and tab search, and survives a restart. An empty name brings the title back.

See every key

ctrl+b ? lists every binding over the page. Press / and type to filter it; Esc or q closes it. Keys changed in your config.toml are marked *.

Firefox's own keys

Most of them still work: ctrl+t, ctrl+shift+t, ctrl+tab, alt+1…alt+9 (cmd+1…cmd+9 on macOS), ctrl+l, ctrl+f and the rest. They are Vimma bindings with Firefox's meaning, so ctrl+b ? lists them and config.toml can rebind them. The few that sit on keys Vimma uses are switched off, so no key means two things: close a tab with x rather than ctrl+w, and open Vimma's bookmarks with ctrl+b shift+b. Firefox's shortcuts has the list, and how to change it.
