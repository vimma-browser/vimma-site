Tabs, panes and workspaces are driven by ctrl+b, tmux's prefix: press ctrl+b, let go, then press one more key. Firefox's tab strip is hidden; the status bar shows where you are (tab 2/5), and ctrl+b g lists every tab when you need to see them.

How the prefix works

  1. Press ctrl+b and let go. The status bar shows prefix.
  2. Press the command key, such as c for a new tab.

There is no time limit between the two, and Esc cancels. The prefix works in every mode, in the URL bar and while you type in a field, and the page never sees it. Prefer ctrl+a? Set prefix in config.toml.

Tab keys

ToPress
Open a new tabctrl+b c or t
Next / previous tabctrl+b n / ctrl+b p, or gt / gT
Go to tab 1 to 9ctrl+b 1 … ctrl+b 9, or 3gt for tab 3
Close the tabctrl+b shift+x or x
Rename the tabctrl+b shift+t
List every tabctrl+b g or b

t, gt, x and b are the normal-mode shortcuts; the ctrl+b forms work everywhere. Tab numbers and next / previous stay within the current workspace.

Find a tab

ctrl+b g (or b) lists the tabs of every workspace in the window. Move with j and k and press Enter, or press / and type a few letters of the title or address to narrow the list. Esc or q closes it. Picking a tab in another workspace takes you there.

Rename a tab

ctrl+b shift+t asks for a name at the bottom of the window. The name replaces the page title in the status bar and the tab list, and survives a restart. An empty name brings the title back.

See every key

ctrl+b ? lists every binding over the page. Press / and type to filter it; Esc or q closes it. Keys your config.toml changes are marked *.

Firefox's own keys

Most still work: ctrl+t, ctrl+shift+t, ctrl+tab, alt+1…alt+9, ctrl+l, ctrl+f and the rest. Most are Vimma bindings with Firefox's meaning, so ctrl+b ? lists them and config.toml can rebind them. The few on keys Vimma uses are off, so a key never means two things: close a tab with x rather than ctrl+w, and find Vimma's bookmarks on ctrl+b shift+b. Firefox's shortcuts has the list, and how to change it.
