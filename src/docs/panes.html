Split the window to see up to four pages side by side. Each page in the split is a pane, and tmux's keys after ctrl+b move between them.

Pane keys

ToPress
Split right / downctrl+b v / ctrl+b -
Split along the longer side (a spiral, as in Hyprland)ctrl+b enter
Rearrange into the next layout presetctrl+b space, or :layout tiled
Move to the pane left / below / above / rightctrl+b h j k l, or ctrl+w h … in normal mode
Zoom the pane to the whole window, and backctrl+b z
Close the pane (its tab stays open)ctrl+b x
Swap with the pane left / below / above / rightctrl+b shift+h j k l
Resizectrl+b r, then h j k l; Esc when done

What to expect

  • A new pane opens the start page with the URL bar focused: type where it should go.
  • The focused pane has a coloured border, and the status bar says pane 2/2. Page keys such as j, H and r act on it. Clicking a pane focuses it too.
  • Closing a pane keeps its tab: it is still in the workspace, and ctrl+b g finds it.
  • Four panes at most. Change that with [panes] max in config.toml.
  • Each workspace keeps its own split, and a restart brings it back as you left it.

Tiling layouts

As in tmux, ctrl+b space moves the panes you have into the next preset: even-horizontal, even-vertical, main-vertical, main-horizontal, tiled and dwindle. The status bar names the one you get. No page reloads: each keeps its scroll position, form text and history. In the main-* presets the focused pane is the big one, and each workspace remembers its last preset.

Resizing

ctrl+b r enters resize mode (the badge says RESIZE). l makes the pane wider, h narrower, j taller and k shorter, 5 % at a time; 4l is four steps. Esc, Enter or q leave. Without the mode, ctrl+w >, ctrl+w <, ctrl+w + and ctrl+w - resize one step, as in Vim.
