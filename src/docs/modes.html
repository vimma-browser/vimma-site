Vimma's keys depend on the mode, as in Vim. Most of the time you are in normal mode, where letters are commands: j scrolls, f shows link hints. Click into a text field and you are in insert mode, where letters type. The badge at the left of the status bar always says which mode you are in, and Esc takes you back to normal.

Two kinds of keys

  • Bare keys (j, f, /) depend on the mode. In normal mode they are Vimma's; in insert mode they type.
  • ctrl+b keys work in every mode, even while you type in a field or the URL bar, because ctrl+b never types anything. Press ctrl+b, let go, then press the next key. The status bar shows prefix while it waits; Esc cancels.

Keys Vimma does not use reach the page as usual: a site's own shortcut on s or m still works in normal mode.

Who gets a key. In normal mode a page never sees Vimma's bare keys (j, f, /); keys Vimma does not bind still reach it. Firefox's shortcuts that a page may use, such as ctrl+s in an editor, and Esc in normal mode, go to the page first, as in Firefox, unless the site's Override keyboard shortcuts permission is Block. Reserved keys such as ctrl+t and ctrl+w stay the browser's.

Typing in a page

  • Click a text field, pick it with f, or press i to go back to the one you were last in on the page, or the first (gi: the first one you can see; 3gi: the third). You are in insert mode.
  • Esc leaves the field and puts you back in normal mode. The page does not see that Esc. If the site needed it, to close a pop-up of its own for instance, press Esc once more.
  • On a page with its own keyboard controls, such as a game, i lets every key through to the page until you press Esc.
  • While you type, Vim's editing keys work: ctrl+w deletes a word, ctrl+u the line before the caret, ctrl+h a character. They work in the URL bar and the : line too.
  • I jumps to the URL bar from normal mode, its address selected, ready to type over. Esc brings you back to the page.

Move around a page

In normal mode you move with single keys: no mouse, no modifiers. They work on web pages, about: pages and in the PDF viewer. Every key is in All the keys for this page.

A number before a key repeats it: 5j scrolls five steps.

Follow a link

  1. Press f. A label, one or two letters, appears on every link, button and field you can see.
  2. Type a label's letters. A link is followed, a button pressed, a text field focused.

gF does the same for links only, and opens the one you pick in a new background tab, so you can pick several in turn. While labels show, Backspace undoes a letter and Esc cancels. Things you cannot see (off screen, or under a cookie banner) get no label: scroll first.

Find text

  1. Press / and type. Matches light up as you type, and the status bar counts them.
  2. Press Enter to stay on the match, or Esc to go back to where you were.
  3. n and N go to the next and the previous match.

A search typed in lower case ignores case; a single capital letter makes the whole search case-sensitive. ? searches backwards.

Copy text

Copy mode selects and copies text from a page with Vim's motions.

  1. Press v. A caret appears at the first text you can see, and the badge says VISUAL.
  2. Move the caret to where your text starts: j and k by line, w and b by word, or f and a character to jump to it on the line.
  3. Press v to start the selection (V for whole lines), then move to extend it.
  4. Press y. The text is on the clipboard, and you are back in normal mode.

Esc leaves without copying. Selecting with the mouse works too: drag or double-click, then press y to copy, or use the motions to extend the selection. Text inside a text field is not reachable from copy mode: click the field and select there as usual.

Copy a paragraph

For a whole paragraph, heading, list item or code block, press F: a label appears on each block of text you can see. Type one, and the block is outlined (the badge says ELEMENT). y copies its text, v selects it in copy mode so you can adjust the selection first, and j / k move to the next or previous block. Esc leaves.

The command line

: opens a Vim-style command line at the bottom of the window. Type a command, press Enter to run it or Esc to cancel. Tab completes, and Up brings back what you ran before. Some to start with:

CommandWhat it does
:open example.comOpens the address in this tab; other words are a web search.
:tabnew example.comThe same, in a new tab.
:qCloses the pane, or the tab when there is one pane.
:workspace workSwitches to the workspace called work.
:helpLists every key, as ctrl+b ? does.

The full list is under Details. Any action on the keys page runs by its name too, such as :scroll_down.

If keys seem to do nothing

  1. Look at the badge. INSERT means a text field has focus: press Esc.
  2. Settings (about:preferences) focuses its search box when it opens, so it starts in insert mode: press Esc before j or k.
  3. Extension pages, data: and blob: pages have no Vim keys: Vimma does not reach them. ctrl+b keys and chords such as ctrl+t still work there. More in Known limits.
