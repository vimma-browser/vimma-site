A workspace is a set of tabs with its own cookies and logins, like a separate browser profile one key away. Keep work in one and personal browsing in another: signing in to a site in one does not sign you in to it in the other.

Under the hood each workspace is a Firefox container. ctrl+b ( and ctrl+b ) step through them, as tmux's session keys do. A window shows one workspace at a time; the others' tabs are hidden, not closed, and everything comes back after a restart.

Make your first workspace

  1. Press ctrl+b shift+n. The command line opens with :wsnew typed for you.
  2. Type a name, such as work, and press Enter. You are now in it: the status bar shows ● 2:work.
  3. Sign in to something. Press ctrl+b ( to go back to main: you are not signed in there.
  4. ctrl+b ) takes you forward to work again.

Good to know

  • Closing a workspace you made deletes its cookies, logins and site data. Vimma asks first; press y to close it, anything else to keep it.
  • Moving a tab reopens it in the other workspace: it keeps its address, but loses its back/forward history and anything typed into forms.
  • New tabs open in the workspace you are in. A link you click opens in the page's own workspace, even in a new tab.
  • Tab numbers, ctrl+b n / ctrl+b p and closing tabs stay within the workspace; ctrl+b g lists every workspace's tabs.
  • If you think of a workspace as a Vim buffer, the keys follow: :wa adds one, :wd closes one, as :badd and :bd do. On layouts where ^ is a dead key, use ctrl+6.
  • Each workspace has its own bookmarks folder.
  • You can declare workspaces in config.toml, and send sites or apps to one automatically: see Routing.
