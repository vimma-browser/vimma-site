Start here

Vimma is a browser for the way you already work. Bare keys drive the page, as in Vim. ctrl+b, tmux's default prefix, drives tabs, panes and workspaces. Your hands stay on the keys, so reading, searching and switching context keep the same flow as your editor and terminal. These pages take you through it in the order you will meet it.

Pre-alpha

Vimma 0.1.1 is released for Linux x86_64: the install page has the one command for Arch Linux and the packages for other distributions. Everything described here works in that release. What does not is on the known limits page, and each topic links to its own.

Pick your path

  • New to Vim keys

    Start with Modes and Moving around. Five keys cover most reading: j k to scroll, f to follow a link, / to find, Esc to get back.

  • Coming from tmux

    The prefix is the same ctrl+b: after it, c, n, p, v, z and w do what they do in tmux, and ( ) step through workspaces as they step through sessions. Start at The prefix and tabs, then Split panes.

  • Coming from Vimium or Tridactyl

    The page keys will feel familiar, and here they also work in the URL bar, on about: pages and in the PDF viewer. Firefox is underneath, so your add-ons come along, now on ctrl+b e. What is new is the window: workspaces, panes and the status bar.

How keys are written

  • ctrl+b v: press ctrl+b, let go, then press v. The status bar shows prefix while it waits.
  • gg: two presses of g. G is shift+g.
  • A count before a key repeats it, as in Vim: 3j scrolls three steps.

Every key is yours

The defaults follow Vim and tmux, but they are a starting point. Every binding and the ctrl+b prefix can be remapped in ~/.config/vimma/config.toml, by the action names on the keys page (an example). Vimma writes a commented file on its first launch, picks up each save, and keeps your last working setup if a save has a mistake. The whole format is on the config.toml page.
